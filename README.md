# VR-Gloves-Report
Report for the VR-gloves thesis project     
<div align="center">Introduction </div> <br>

<div align="center">Thesis </div><br>

How can VR gloves work to promote immersive and responsive Virtual Reality gameplay? The big thing that makes VR special is due to the unreachable level of immersion you get while using it compared to normal desktop use. I have loved VR for nearly a decade and though huge leaps have happened in terms of performance and better specs nothing has improved immersion directly in VR for a long time that is for the average consumer. Some corporate alternative exists that does improve immersion whether it's treadmills so you can walk around or by improving touch with vests and or gloves. all of these usually costs thousands of dollars and most is not made for the average person. I wanted to create something that elevates immersion to a level not really seen or meant for the average person before. There are multiple point that needs to be checked, affordability, immersion increase worth the price and lastly as effortless to make as possible.
<br>

<div align="center">Research </div><br>
I got inspiration to my project because of my enjoyment for VR media and after the disconnection of the biggest investor in VR Meta I wanted to give an example on how to take a step forward within the VR industry or maybe just check if this is a viable option to move forward. I wanted to make a VR Demo Game as well to show an example on how these gloves might be put to use and the difference, but something I learned was that the project got to big to handle and it just won't fit within an 8 week schedule so I just opted to use existing games to showcase the use of Haptics in VR. 
<br>

There are a lot of different haptics that you can have on gloves so it's best to narrow it down to what's doable instead of having an impossible task in front of you. From the high budget gloves meant for corporations they typically have finger tracking, resistive feedback, vibration feedback to simulate touch and pressure feedback also simulate touch by adding pressure in the form of objects in your palm. The most logical to do was resistive feedback and finger tracking since both of those were a part of gloves that was heavily researched before by the youtuber Lucas VRTech. Some other alternatives existed like vibration haptics but there wasn't an easy way of fusing this with the other haptics that was both reliable and worth it. Resistive feedback was deemed to be more immersive so that took presidence.
<br>

I chose to use software and parts that was recommended by Lucas VRTech which is a youtuber and software engineer that had the most detailed and perhaps the only self made full scale gloves that have some sort of resistive feedback. This is how I knew that it was doable. But it was quite the challenge mostly due to on how to do the wiring. It was a complete different set of skills that I have no experience in, so I got to research on how to use crimping tools, how to use breadboards and a substantial amount of electrical skills and wiring methods more. The most difficult was to choose which option was the best one. The fear of missing the best option on this part was quite severe. Soldering seemed to be a avid part of a lot of these methods but since I have no experience in soldering and it's difficult level plus the risk of damaging my limited amount of potentiometers and motors, they could be purchased again but the valuable time i have left is not worth the risk, crimping was the safest choice. 
<br>

I learned that you cannot think about what action is the best one for to long, the Fear of missing out wasted quite a bit of my time during this project and would be something I would change for other project in the future gaming related or not.
